Simulation Modes

dN/dt = rN × (K − N) / K
Key idea: when N is far below K, growth is rapid. As N approaches K, the factor (K − N)/K becomes smaller, so population growth slows.

Exponential vs Logistic Growth

Both models start with the same N₀ and r. The logistic model includes the carrying-capacity term, while the exponential model assumes unlimited resources.

What should you observe?

  • The graph forms an S-shaped curve when the population begins below K.
  • Increasing r makes the population rise more quickly, but does not change the carrying capacity.
  • Increasing K raises the population level around which the curve eventually stabilises.
  • Growth is fastest around the middle of the S-curve, near N = K/2.

Assumptions & Limitations

  • K is treated as constant throughout the simulation.
  • The population is represented by one overall growth rate r.
  • All individuals are assumed to experience the same environmental conditions.
  • Real ecosystems can have changing resources, migration, disease, predation, competition and seasonal effects that this simple model does not include.
